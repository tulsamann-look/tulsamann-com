# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two primary cohorts, weighted roughly 50 / 50.

**Cohort A — Prospective Look Consulting clients.**
B2B founders and ops leaders who land here because Tulsa cold-emailed them or because someone referred him. They already know about Look (or are about to find out) and they're searching his name to verify he's real before they reply. Their context is a 30-second background-check tab, opened between meetings. They want signal: is this someone I'd hire to run lead gen for me? They're not coming to read essays. They're coming to confirm Tulsa exists, builds things, and knows what he's doing — then either book a call, save the tab for later, or close it.

**Cohort B — AI / Claude-curious peers, operators, and content followers.**
People who found Tulsa through his AI content, his Claude Partner Network positioning, or someone in the Claude implementation space pointing at him. They include other consultants, AI-curious founders, agency operators, and lurkers from his writing. Their context is "I want to read what this person thinks about Claude and AI in business." They're here for the thinking, the work-in-public, the receipts. Some will become Cohort A over time — they buy after they trust the thinking — but their first visit is for substance, not pitch.

The site must serve both without compromising either. A site that funnels Cohort B into "book a Look call" alienates them. A site that buries the Look CTA loses Cohort A.

## Product Purpose

A founder-operator brand site that runs two surfaces in parallel: a **business surface** that converts inbound interest in Tulsa-the-operator into Look Consulting engagements, and a **thinking surface** that compounds Tulsa-the-builder's reputation in the Claude implementation and AI-for-business space. The two surfaces share infrastructure (one site, one navigation, one personality) but route to different outcomes.

Success looks like: a visitor leaves in 60 seconds knowing what Tulsa does and having taken a clear next step — either booking a call (Cohort A), reading or subscribing to a piece of writing (Cohort B), or both. The site is the durable home base that cold email, LinkedIn, podcast appearances, and Claude Partner Network activity all point at. It is not a portfolio in the showcase sense; it is a reputation layer that compounds.

## Brand Personality

**Opinionated. Fast. Calm.**

- **Opinionated** — The site has takes. About Look's work, about Claude implementation, about how lead gen actually works inside SMBs. No hedging, no "every business is different" filler, no consultant-speak. Strong voice, first-person, specific.
- **Fast** — Visitors get what they came for in seconds. Tight copy. No throat-clearing intros. Every section earns its space. No "Welcome to my site" hero. Information density is high; decoration is low.
- **Calm** — The site never shouts. No animated gradients, no auto-playing video, no "BOOK A FREE STRATEGY CALL NOW" stakes-raising. Confidence is communicated through restraint. The reader trusts the operator more for not being shouted at.

Voice: first-person, plain English, specific over abstract. Sentences are short. Numbers and proper nouns earn the reader's attention. "Cut Charterhouse Lombard's cost-per-lead in half over 90 days" not "delivered transformative results for our clients."

## Anti-references

**SaaS startup landing slop (hard rule).**
- No purple gradients, gradient orbs, mesh backgrounds.
- No "Trusted by" logo walls used as the home-page hero.
- No AI-rendered hero illustrations of abstract people pointing at floating UI.
- No "The future of [X]" headlines.
- No stock-photo team grids.
- No generic Inter at every weight as the only typeface.
- No "Get started for free" button in the top-right when there's nothing to get started with.

In short: nothing that signals "AI-generated portfolio template, designed in 30 minutes by someone who's never shipped." This is the single most important visual rule. Everything else can be negotiated.

## Design Principles

These are strategic, not visual. They apply across copy, layout, content priority, and CTA placement.

1. **Two paths, equal weight.** The home page serves Cohort A and Cohort B without picking a winner. There is a clear "work with Tulsa / Look" path AND a clear "read what Tulsa thinks" path. Neither is buried, neither is shouted. Visitors self-route.

2. **Receipts beside every claim.** Every line that says "I do X" sits next to proof that I did X. Claim about Look's lead-gen results → next to a CHL or EE case-study link with a real metric. Claim about Claude implementation → next to a shipped lead magnet, a partner-network credential, or a written breakdown. No floating credentials.

3. **Read like a builder, not an agency.** First-person ("I built…"), specific (named clients, named tools, real numbers), unpolished where polish would feel performative. The site should read like Tulsa wrote it on a Sunday after shipping something, not like a marketing team approved it. No "we" pronoun (this is one person), no agency-corporate boilerplate.

4. **Engagement before delight.** Every section answers "what's the next step for this visitor?" before "is this beautiful?". CTAs are persistent, contextual, and honest about what happens next ("Book a 30-minute call about lead gen" not "Get in touch"). Visual delight is allowed and welcome — but only after the engagement architecture is solid.

5. **Substance density over visual surface.** Light mode is chosen because business-owner readers need cognitive ease, not because minimal looks good. The site is information-rich and decoration-poor. Whitespace earns its space by surrounding signal, not by replacing it. A page that scrolls four screens of beautiful nothing fails this principle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA** as the floor. AAA where it costs nothing extra (line-height, body type contrast).
- **Reduced motion respected.** All non-essential animation gates on `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. The site must function and feel calm with motion disabled — that is the natural state of the brand anyway.
- **Color blindness.** No information conveyed through color alone. Status, links, and CTAs are differentiated by weight, underline, or shape in addition to color.
- **Keyboard navigable end-to-end.** Visible focus rings, logical tab order, no keyboard traps.
- **Body type minimum 16px** (and likely 18px+ for long-form writing routes).
- **Reading audience is global** — assume English-as-second-language readers in the Look-prospect cohort. Plain language, short sentences, no idioms that don't travel.
